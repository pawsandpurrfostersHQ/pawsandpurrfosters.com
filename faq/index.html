Frequently Asked Questions

Have questions about our foster kittens or the adoption process? Here are a few helpful answers. All adoptions are completed through No Paws Left Behind Kitty Rescue.

Do bonded pairs need to be adopted together?

Yes. Kittens labeled as a bonded pair must be adopted together, regardless of age. Some kittens may be listed as a preferred pair or may be able to go home with one of their siblings. In special cases, a singleton may be adopted on their own or may need a home with another cat. Please read each kitten’s profile for their specific pairing requirements. In addition, No Paws Left Behind Kitty Rescue’s policy requires kittens six months old and younger to be adopted in pairs. Most of our kittens will need to go home with a companion.

Can I meet a kitten before applying?

Yes! You can meet a kitten before applying as long as their profile lists them as Available. Visit our Events & Locations page to see where we will be next and learn about upcoming meet-and-greet opportunities.

Where do adoption events take place?

Adoption events are generally held on weekends, with occasional special events at other locations. Schedules can change, so please check our Events & Locations page for the current schedule and special-event details.

How do I apply to adopt?

There are two ways to get an application. You can visit No Paws Left Behind Kitty Rescue’s contact page and ask the rescue to send you the appropriate application and instructions. Or, you can visit the rescue’s adoption forms page to download either an editable application to complete electronically or a printable copy to fill out by hand and bring to an in-person event. If completing it electronically, email the application to Follow the rescue’s instructions for submitting the completed electronic application. The email address should be confirmed directly with the rescue before sending.

For the full process, visit How to Adopt.

Who handles the adoption?

We are a foster team working in partnership with No Paws Left Behind Kitty Rescue. The kittens are placed in our home through the rescue. All adoption applications, application reviews and approvals, contracts, adoption fees, and final adoptions are handled by the rescue. Our role is to provide a safe, loving foster home, get to know each kitten, and help you find the right match for your home.

What comes home with an adopted kitten?

When the adoption is finalized and your kitten is ready to go home, they will come with a blue take-home folder containing their spay or neuter paperwork, vaccine card, microchip number and registration information, foster contact information, and a kitten care guide. The folder also includes an adoption certificate and a Pet Food Express coupon. Adoptions completed at PetSmart may include a PetSmart coupon booklet.

What if I’m interested in a kitten who isn’t ready yet?

Kittens labeled Coming Soon or Almost Available are not currently available for adoption and are not accepting adoption applications. Coming Soon kittens are often still very young and are growing, developing their personalities, and learning in foster care. Almost Available kittens are getting closer, but still need more time before they can be listed as Available. Please check back for updates. No kitten is promised or reserved based on an inquiry.

Can I apply for a kitten listed as Pending Interest?

Yes. An application is currently being considered, and the applicant may be completing the adoption process. Other applicants may still apply. If the current applicant does not move forward, another applicant may have the opportunity to be considered. The rescue works on a first-come, first-served basis.

What does Returned — Available mean?

These kittens were previously adopted and have since returned to foster care. They may need extra patience and time to settle back into their familiar routine. We generally allow around a week to a month for this adjustment, although it can be shorter or longer depending on the kitten. As their foster parent, I observe how they are settling in and let the rescue know when I feel they are ready to return to adoption events. Every kitten is different, and a returned kitten may benefit from a patient home and a little extra understanding as they adjust to a new environment again.

Still have questions? Visit our How to Adopt page or contact No Paws Left Behind Kitty Rescue for adoption-process questions.
